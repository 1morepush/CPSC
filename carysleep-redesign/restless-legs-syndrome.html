Restless Legs Syndrome

About 3 min read

Key points

  • Restless legs syndrome (RLS) causes an uncomfortable feeling in the legs and a strong urge to move them.
  • It is usually worse in the evening and at night, especially when you are lying down or sitting still, and moving your legs brings relief.
  • It can make it hard to fall asleep and stay asleep.
  • Once it is diagnosed, treatment helps many people.

What is restless legs syndrome?

RLS is a common condition of the nervous system. People describe the feeling in many ways: crawling, tingling, prickling, pulling or aching deep inside the legs. It is hard to describe, but it comes with a strong urge to move. Walking, stretching or rubbing the legs helps for a while.

Most people notice the symptoms less during the day and more in the evening or at night, especially as they are trying to fall asleep. For many, the symptoms fade by early morning, which is when they finally get their most refreshing sleep.

Signs to look for

  • An urge to move your legs, usually with uncomfortable feelings in them
  • Symptoms that start or get worse when you rest, lie down or sit for a long time, such as in the car or at the movies
  • Relief when you move, walk or stretch
  • Symptoms that are worse in the evening or at night
  • Trouble falling asleep, and feeling tired during the day

Many people with RLS also have leg jerks or kicks while they sleep, which can wake them or their bed partner.

What causes it?

Often there is no clear cause, and RLS can run in families. It is also linked to:

  • Low iron levels in the body
  • Pregnancy, especially the last few months
  • Kidney disease
  • Nerve damage in the legs (neuropathy), for example from diabetes
  • Some medicines, such as certain allergy, anti-nausea and antidepressant medicines

How is it diagnosed?

There is no single test for RLS. Your doctor diagnoses it from your description of the symptoms and an exam. Blood tests can check your iron levels and look for other causes. If your sleep is badly disturbed, a sleep study may be suggested to look for leg movements during sleep and other sleep problems.

How is it treated?

Treatment depends on how often symptoms happen and how much they affect your sleep.

  • Treating the cause. If your iron is low, iron supplements may help. Changing a medicine that makes RLS worse can also help. Don't stop any medicine without talking to your doctor first.
  • Medicines. Once diagnosed, many patients find that medicine relieves their symptoms. Your doctor will help choose the right one.

Things you can do at home

  • Keep a regular sleep schedule.
  • Get regular, moderate exercise, but not right before bed.
  • Cut back on caffeine, alcohol and nicotine, especially in the evening.
  • Try a warm bath, leg massage or gentle stretching before bed.
  • Keep your mind busy when you have to sit still, for example with a book or puzzle.

If restless legs are keeping you up at night, talk to us. Better sleep is possible.
